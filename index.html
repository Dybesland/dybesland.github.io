Sara Dybesland

PhD Researcher in Political Science
European University Institute, Florence

I study (un)equal political representation, political parties, legislative behaviour and gender. My research asks whether the issues that matter most to citizens are equally reflected in parties' and representatives' political agendas at different stages of the policymaking process. I use and teach a variety of quantitative methods, including regression analysis, large-scale text analysis, machine learning and survey experiments.

During my PhD at the Department of Political and Social Sciences at the European University Institute, I did a research stay at the Department of Comparative Politics at the University of Bergen and a visit to the DYNAMICS Research Training Group at Humboldt University of Berlin.

Working papers

Why she cares about caring: explaining gender differences in political priorities
Sara Helene Dybesland

Using two decades of cross-national survey data on Europeans’ views of the most important political issues, the study examines gender differences in political priorities and how these vary over time and across countries. Focusing on health and social security, issues that are frequently assumed to be of special importance to women, the analysis reveals that women are, on average, more likely to prioritize health and social security as a most important issue, however gendered patterns of issue salience are highly context-dependent. In particular, the gender segregation of the labor market seems to shape these dynamics: in contexts where women are more concentrated in health and care sectors, the gender gap in prioritizing health and social security is significantly larger. These findings underscore the need to explore how structural contexts and intersectional identities shape public opinion.

Manifesto? Gendered Patterns of Voter-Party Priority Congruence in Western Europe
Sara Helene Dybesland

Do parties’ electoral programs represent men’s and women’s issue priorities equally well? This paper asks whether priority congruence is gender biased, that is, whether parties devote equal attention to the issues that men and women consider most important. I address this question by combining two decades of Eurobarometer data (2002–2023), covering 473,793 respondents’ “most important issue” answers, with 560 party manifestos from 66 elections in 14 Western European countries. Using a keyword-assisted topic model (KeyATM) on Comparative Manifesto Project texts, I estimate party attention to twelve issue categories that mirror the Eurobarometer items and link these party-level topic proportions to individual citizens’ stated priorities.

I distinguish between systemic congruence (between citizens and the full party supply) and congruence between citizens and their ideologically closest party. Preliminary results show that, at the systemic level, women’s most important issues receive slightly more attention from parties than men’s. These gender gaps are larger where women’s parliamentary representation is higher and party systems are more fragmented, but they do not systematically vary with parties’ left–right orientation. For closest-party congruence, women’s priorities also tend to receive more attention, but this advantage is conditioned by voters’ ideological placement. The gender gap in closest-party congruence is not present among voters on the far left but becomes pronounced in women’s favor as voters place themselves further to the right.

Do Politicians Stereotype Their Voters? Gendered Issue Ownership and Democratic Responsiveness
Sara Dybesland and Jana Belschner

Using an elite survey experiment with elected representatives in Norway, we test whether politicians are more willing to put an issue on the agenda when the constituent raising it has a gender that stereotypically "owns" that issue.
